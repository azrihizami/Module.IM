Wind the coils

Twelve pulls on the winding machine: 130 turns, two ties, and the tray in insertion order.

    Chapters
    In short

    What to take from this lecture

    • Set the former once, before the first pull: pillars at 100 mm, and a trial turn of about 315 mm on the bare step.
    • Every pull is the same: thread, wind 130 turns (slow, fast, slow), tie on the former, cut and sleeve, release, lay it out.
    • Tie before the coil comes off: an untied coil springs open and the turns cross.
    • Lay each coil on its tray place in insertion order, start lead towards you, and measure about 3 Ω.
    • Weigh the reel before and after: the twelve coils take about 1.18 kg, some 520 m of wire.

    Every word of the lecture

    Show the transcript

    0:00Wind the coils

    Lecture three. Wind the coils. In the last lecture, we planned twelve coils and worked out the former setting. Today we wind them on the winding machine, one pull at a time, and lay each coil on the tray, ready for the stator.

    0:17The plan: 12 pulls, 130 turns

    Here is the plan in the Winding Workshop. Twelve pulls, all the same. One hundred and thirty turns of zero point five six millimetre wire. The thirty-six millimetre step, with the pillars one hundred millimetres between centres.

    0:32Safety at the machine

    First, safety at the machine. Hair tied back, no loose sleeves, and no gloves near the turning former. Eye protection when you cut wire. And stop the machine before you touch the wire. Every time.

    0:46Set the former

    Before the first pull, weigh the reel and write down your prediction of the mass you will use. Then set the former. With the machine switched off, slide the pillars to one hundred millimetres between centres and lock the wing nuts. Wind one scrap turn on the step, take it off and measure it. You want about three hundred and fifteen millimetres. But the mean turn is three hundred and thirty millimetres. So why is the trial turn shorter? Pause and think. Because the trial turn sits on the bare step. The mean turn sits halfway up the finished bundle, which is about five and a half millimetres high.

    1:31Thread the wire

    Step two: thread the wire. Bring it through the tensioner and the guide. Leave a start lead of two hundred millimetres, slip on its sleeve, and clamp the lead under the anchor screw. Later, that lead becomes a joint, or a terminal.

    1:48Wind 130 turns

    Step three: wind. Preset the counter to one hundred and thirty. Wind the first five turns slowly, so the first layer lies flat; every layer above it follows the first. Then wind faster, with even tension, and slow down again for the last ten.

    2:06Tie the coil

    Step four: tie the coil, while it is still on the former. Machine off, and keep the wire taut, so the last turn cannot unwind. Two ties, one on each straight side. What happens if you take a coil off without tying it? Pause and think. It springs open, and the turns cross. Then you wind it again.

    2:34Cut, release, take it off

    Step five: cut the wire two hundred millimetres past the coil, and slip on the end sleeve. Step six: unclamp the start lead from the anchor screw, release the pillars, and take the coil off. Hold it by the straight sides, not by the ends. Then slide the pillars back to one hundred millimetres for the next coil.

    2:56Tag, measure, tray place 1

    Step seven: tag the coil and lay it on tray place one, start lead towards you. Scrape the enamel off the lead tips, and measure the resistance: about three ohms at twenty degrees, a little more in a warm workshop. The tray follows the insertion order, so the first coil you wind is the first to go in.

    3:16The other eleven coils

    Now the other eleven coils, in card order, all wound the same way round. Check the count before each coil comes off the former. Before you weigh the reel again, predict it: how much lighter will it be after twelve coils? Pause and think.

    3:39The full tray

    Twelve coils on the tray. Weigh the reel again. The mass you lost is the wire in the winding: about one point one eight kilograms, about five hundred and twenty metres.

    3:51Recap

    So: thread, wind, tie, cut, release, and lay it out. Then the same again, eleven more times. Before the next lecture, record the reel mass and the turn counts in worksheet four point three, part B.
