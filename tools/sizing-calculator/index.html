How to use this calculator

  1. Start from the example. The boxes already hold an example: a B-segment range-extended EV. “Reset to example values” brings them back at any time.
  2. Change your design choices. Peak power, ratio, CPSR, shear stress, rotor diameter and poles are yours. Vehicle data and targets describe the example car. Replace them with your own vehicle, and write down where each number came from.
  3. Read the checks. Each target and limit shows Pass or Fail. The design card sums up the motor you have sized.
  4. Save the design card at the bottom of the page to keep your numbers and open them again later.

Press ? next to a box to see what it is, what it changes and its starting value.

    Method

    What the calculator does

    • Acceleration is simulated in 1 ms steps: motor torque is the lesser of peak torque and peak power over speed, and drops to zero at maximum motor speed.
    • The hand estimate in table A uses the textbook formula with the base vehicle speed from your ratio and CPSR.
    • Stack length is rounded up to the next 5 mm and stator OD to the nearest 5 mm.
    • Turns per phase and flux linkage are first estimates for the voltage budget. The winding design and the finite-element check refine them.
    • Your inputs are remembered in this browser only.
    The example case

    A B-segment range-extended EV

    The starting values describe a small range-extended car: about 1,300 kg on test and 1,600 kg laden, driven only by its traction motor through a single-speed gear. The targets below were set so that the design problem is well defined. They are Training targets, not the specification of any production vehicle.
