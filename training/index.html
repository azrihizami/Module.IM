EV Traction Motor Sizing and Design

A course for automotive and motor engineers who specify, source or design traction motors. It follows one running case, a range-extended B-segment car, from vehicle targets to a motor checked in FEMM.

The car around the motor

Every other part of the powertrain answers to the motor

Swipe the diagram sideways to see the whole powertrain.

  1. 1Wheels and roadS1Road load and the vehicle targets set the motor’s peak and continuous torque, speed and power.
  2. 2Reduction gearS1The ratio trades motor torque for speed and fixes the maximum motor speed and frequency.
  3. 3Traction inverterS4Motor current, power factor and back-EMF size the inverter, its devices and its fault protection.
  4. 4DC busS1, S5The bus voltage caps the motor’s back-EMF, which sets its turns and magnet flux linkage.
  5. 5Battery packS0Motor and inverter efficiency over the drive cycle sets the kWh needed for a given range.
  6. 6Cooling loopS3, S4Motor and inverter losses set the coolant flow, the radiator and the continuous rating.
  7. 7Engine-generatorS1The power the motor draws on a long climb or at top speed sizes the range extender.
  8. 8Fuel tankS0With the battery depleted, drive efficiency sets litres per 100 km, and so the tank for a given range.
Why it matters

Range is decided in the motor

A carmaker sells range: a good figure on the test cycle, delivered with the smallest battery and fuel tank possible. Between the road and the battery sits the electric drive. The traction motor is sized for its peak torque, but on the test cycle it spends almost all its time at light load, and its efficiency there sets how many watt-hours each kilometre costs.

The course opens with this, then makes every design decision with its price in kilowatt-hours.
